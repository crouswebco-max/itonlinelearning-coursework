# 2.8 Cloud Economics

## CapEx and OpEx

| | **CapEx** (capital expenditure) | **OpEx** (operational expenditure) |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Big up-front purchases of physical things | Ongoing, pay-as-you-go spending |
| **Example** | Buying servers for a data centre | Your monthly AWS bill |
| **Cloud effect** | The cloud **reduces** it | The cloud **moves spending here** |

This is advantage no. 1: **trade fixed expense (CapEx) for variable expense (OpEx).**

## Total cost of ownership (TCO)

**TCO** compares the *full* cost of running something yourself with running it in the cloud.

**On-premises costs people forget:**
- Server, storage and network hardware
- Data centre space, power and cooling
- Physical security
- IT staff to maintain it all
- Software licences
- Replacing hardware every few years

**Cloud:** many of these become AWS's job and are built into the price.

## Ways to save money in the cloud

| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Rightsizing** | Pick the smallest resource that does the job properly |
| **Elasticity and Auto Scaling** | Only run what's needed right now |
| **Turn things off** | Stop development servers at night and at weekends |
| **Pricing models** | Savings Plans and Reserved Instances (cheaper for steady use), Spot Instances (much cheaper, but can be interrupted). These are covered properly in Domain 4 |
| **Managed services** | Less staff time spent on maintenance |
| **Licensing** | Bring your own licence (**BYOL**), or use licence-included instances |

## 2.8.2 Practical: the AWS Pricing Calculator

The **[AWS Pricing Calculator](https://calculator.aws/)** estimates costs *before* you build anything. It's free and needs no sign-in.

1. Go to **calculator.aws** and click **Create estimate**.
2. Search for **Amazon EC2** and click **Configure**.
3. Choose a **Region** (for example Europe (London)).
4. Pick an instance type, for example `t3.micro` (2 vCPUs and 1 GiB of memory).
5. Set usage to **24 hours a day** and compare the pricing models: **On-Demand** against a **Savings Plan**.
6. Click **Save and add service**, then add **Amazon S3** storage (for example 50 GB of S3 Standard).
7. Look at the **monthly and 12-month totals**, then click **Share** to get a link, or **Export** to CSV or PDF.

**Things to notice:**
- The same instance costs different amounts in different **Regions**.
- Committing with a **Savings Plan** or **Reserved Instance** lowers the hourly price.
